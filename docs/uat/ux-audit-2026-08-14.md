# OpenEscrow mission-driven UX audit

Date: 2026-08-14

Status: active remediation audit

Canonical product: `https://openescrow.io/`
Data boundary: synthetic Base Sepolia data only

## North star

OpenEscrow should let a landlord and every tenant complete the same rental-deposit
lifecycle with less ambiguity than email, spreadsheets, or disconnected payment
records. At every step, a participant should understand:

1. what happened;
2. what needs their attention now;
3. what amount and deadline apply;
4. what is private versus public;
5. what action is safe to take next; and
6. where the shared record can be checked later.

Technical success is not enough. A transaction that succeeds while the interface
shows a red error, hides the next action, uses the wrong role language, or produces
an incomplete Record fails the product outcome.

## Method and evidence

The review combines:

- rendered inspection of the public landing, Help, Funding, Privacy, Terms, Demo,
  landlord, tenant, proposal, deposit, claim, and Record surfaces;
- the owner's supervised three-account lifecycle observations;
- DOM, overflow, accessibility, and browser-harness checks at phone, tablet, and
  desktop sizes;
- source tracing across the React client, Worker API, D1 model, email delivery,
  Base Sepolia receipt verification, and smart contracts; and
- comparison of visible UI state with the expected role, deadline, value, and
  lifecycle invariants.

This audit does not treat a mocked component, provider `delivered` status, or a
successful transaction receipt as proof that the complete user outcome passed.

## Priority model

- **S0 — stop:** value loss, privacy breach, destructive ambiguity, or unsafe
  real-money behavior.
- **S1 — release blocker:** a core lifecycle, role boundary, amount, deadline, or
  shared record is materially wrong.
- **S2 — major:** the workflow is usable only with avoidable confusion or a fragile
  workaround.
- **S3 — moderate:** localized clarity, responsive, accessibility, or visual
  hierarchy issue.
- **S4 — polish:** cosmetic improvement with no material effect on completion.

## Findings and recommended improvements

| ID | Surface | Finding and user risk | Recommendation | Severity | State |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UX-001 | Settlement and withdrawal | A landlord or tenant could be invited to withdraw before the agreed claim period ends. That can defeat the documented claim window. | Enforce the same unlock timestamp in the contract, receipt API, notification scheduler, and disabled-action copy. Keep cancellation refunds separate from ordinary closure. | S1 | Implemented locally; replacement contract and hosted retest required |
| UX-002 | Record reconciliation | A successful blockchain action could appear as a red private-Record failure or lose actor, token, USD-equivalent, evidence, or terminal-state context. Users cannot trust a record that contradicts the receipt. | Persist exact validated log topics/data and render a consumer-readable indexed activity with explicit chain-only provenance. Keep private-document links separate and permission checked. | S1 | Implemented locally; full hosted reconciliation retest required |
| UX-003 | Yield settlement | A deduction denominated in yield-token shares can transfer tenant-earned yield to the landlord. | Express the claim in principal-equivalent test USD, convert only at settlement, cap the landlord at the principal-equivalent claim, and allocate positive yield to tenants. | S1 | Implemented in the revised candidate; exact cohort retest required |
| UX-004 | Required actions | Proposal approval, funding, claims, responses, and completed outcomes were not consistently represented in **Needs attention** or linked to the exact panel. | Use one role-aware action model for the dashboard count, highlighted card, notification, and deep link. A click should open the relevant proposal or the agreement's Funds or Claims panel in place. | S1 | Core model implemented; adjacent hosted states still need retest |
| UX-005 | Invitations and account discovery | Requiring a special link to rediscover an agreement makes email the only navigation system and creates stale-link confusion. | Bind invitations to verified recipient identity, send a canonical `openescrow.io` entry link, recover authorized current proposals on ordinary sign-in, and retain role-locked tokens only as a secure fallback. | S1 | Implemented; wrong-account, stale-link, and ordinary-sign-in hosted retest required |
| UX-006 | Transaction recovery | Success followed by a receipt-indexing delay was shown as a red failure with a technical event-mismatch message. Users may repeat an irreversible action. | Separate **transaction succeeded** from **Record update pending**. Disable duplicate submission, reconcile automatically, and place any manual recovery under a secondary technical disclosure. | S1 | Receipt paths remediated; hosted RPC-delay and proof retest required |
| UX-007 | Proposal authoring | The long form mixes address discovery, legal timing, asset selection, participant shares, and advanced concepts. | UX-008 | Address search | Correct addresses could require punctuation and full locality details before suggestions appeared. | Normalize punctuation and unit syntax, query progressively after enough street context, show useful locality candidates, and distinguish **suggested** from **validated**. Do not infer jurisdiction from an unconfirmed string. | S2 | Implemented and regression covered; broader address matrix pending |
| UX-009 | Proposal lifecycle language | A newly published proposal could be labelled as a revision or display stale approval warnings. | Derive labels from persisted revision and approval state, not local editor history. Use **Publish proposal** for the first version and **Publish revised proposal** only after a material replacement. | S2 | Implemented; refresh/stale-tab retest pending |
| UX-010 | Invitation delivery | A send button without durable status makes the landlord unsure whether to resend. | Automatically send after publish, show a persistent green `✓ Sent` state per recipient, and offer only **Resend** and **Send manually** fallbacks. | S2 | Implemented; concurrency and reload retest pending |
| UX-011 | Funding | Approve and fund can feel like one action but requires successive onchain transactions; landlord rows also showed irrelevant funded values. | Present one guided tenant action with two explicit transaction stages and progress. Hide tenant-funding metrics from landlord-only participation rows while retaining deduction and available amounts. | S2 | Implemented; wallet-rejection between stages needs retest |
| UX-012 | Claims | The documentation checklist appeared before note/upload inputs; submitted documentation links could be missing or fail cross-origin. | Put note and private upload first, then the attestations the user is confirming. Show an authorized **View supporting document** action in the claim and report, with same-origin permission-checked delivery. | S1 | Implemented; authorized/unauthorized hosted document retest required |
| UX-013 | No-arbiter workflow | Arbitration language dominated parts of Help and claim copy even though the public MVP defaults to a standardized shared record without arbitration. | Keep the standard no-arbiter path primary. Put optional arbiter behavior in a clearly labelled future/advanced test disclosure without changing the underlying legacy capability. | S2 | Public workflow simplified; Help hierarchy polish planned |
| UX-014 | Agreement status | **Finalized** was used for both an active funded deposit and a fully completed lifecycle. | Use distinct visible states: ready to fund, active deposit, active claim, refund ready, and complete. Match Dashboard, Deposits, Record, and report language. | S1 | Implemented; cross-surface terminal retest required |
| UX-015 | Agreement navigation | Summary, Funds & withdrawals, and Claims & resolution were too visually quiet and could scroll off-screen on small displays. | Use equal-width, high-contrast segmented tabs with visible selected, focus, count/attention, and disabled states. Keep all three visible at phone widths. | S2 | Implemented locally; responsive rendered retest pending |
| UX-016 | Record density | Revision tables and technical proof controls created a long, intimidating document. | Collapse revision sections by default, keep the current/final revision most prominent, show USD value first and token amount second, and maintain explicit expand/collapse controls. | S2 | Mostly implemented; dense multi-revision report retest pending |
| UX-017 | Public proof | Step numbering and proof language appeared only after another artifact was generated, and a successful onchain save could still look unsuccessful. | Always show the three-step Record path, label public proof as optional, and separate proof existence from private-Record reconciliation status. | S2 | Implemented locally; hosted save/verify/recovery retest pending |
| UX-018 | Account and role context | A participant could lose track of whether they were acting as landlord or tenant. | Keep a color-coded role chip beside the account name and repeat the active role in action-critical cards. Invitation role lock must not silently become the account's permanent role. | S2 | Implemented; multi-role account scenario pending |
| UX-019 | Signed-out header | At invitation widths, sign-in buttons and legal text could overflow the right edge while the wordmark consumed too much horizontal space. | Let the header switch to a single-column layout before controls clip, constrain the wordmark, wrap actions and consent text, and prevent document-level horizontal overflow. | S1 | Implemented; invitation URLs at 200% zoom need final retest |
| UX-020 | Workspace navigation | Five top-level tabs could be hidden behind horizontal scrolling on phones. | Use a visible multi-row grid at narrow widths, preserve focus order, and show attention counts without relying on a tiny dot alone. | S2 | Implemented locally; authenticated phone retest pending |
| UX-021 | Dashboard summaries | A single **Finalized agreements** count does not explain whether deposits are active, claimed, or refunded. | Keep separate counts for needs attention, active proposals, finalized agreements, active deposits, active claims, and completed refunds, with role-specific explanatory text. | S2 | Implemented; empty and dense-state retest pending |
| UX-022 | Email experience | Links, branding, privacy language, send state, and unsubscribe presentation were inconsistent across event types. | Use the wordmark, a canonical general-app CTA, privacy-minimal body, and a settings-management notice. Keep legally required unsubscribe mechanics available without making an accidental opt-out the primary action. | S2 | Implemented; full event-map inbox/spam rehearsal pending |
| UX-023 | Tables and data-heavy cards | Dense participant, accounting, claim, and report tables are accurate but visually dry and difficult at zoom. | Preserve semantic tables for export and desktop comparison; on small screens use labelled value blocks, stronger section summaries, chips for status, and USD-large/token-small hierarchy. | S3 | Partly implemented; systematic dense-state pass planned |
| UX-024 | Failure language | Some errors expose transaction/event/RPC vocabulary before explaining impact. | Lead with outcome and safety: **Your transaction succeeded; the Record is still catching up.** Put hashes, event names, and recovery diagnostics in a collapsed technical section. | S2 | Partly implemented; copy inventory planned |
| UX-025 | Accessibility | Critical flows need proof at keyboard-only, 200% zoom, visible focus, screen-reader announcement, reduced motion, and dense content states—not just a clean automated scan. | Run the full manual checklist for proposal, approval/funding, claim response, Record download/proof, settings, and every recovery state. | S2 | Automated baseline passes; authenticated manual pass pending |

## Safe improvements included in this milestone

- Claim-period withdrawal gating is aligned across the contract, interface,
  receipts, and scheduled notices.
- Direct Base Sepolia activity retains the validated event payload needed for a
  useful private Record entry rather than only a generic event name.
- Indexed events show plain-language actor, amount, token, evidence, and
  provenance details while remaining honest about chain-only data.
- Workspace navigation and agreement-panel tabs remain visible at narrow widths.
- The Record always presents its complete report, encrypted backup, and optional
  public-proof sequence.
- Direct-chain activity copy uses consumer language first and technical details
  second.

## Deliberately gated work

The following must not be represented as complete through styling alone:

1. **Replacement contract release.** The withdrawal lock and exact settlement
   behavior need the owner's supervised Base Sepolia signature and independent
   runtime/binding verification.
2. **Hosted lifecycle proof.** The exact deployed candidate needs a fresh
   landlord/two-tenant run through invitation, ordinary sign-in discovery,
   approval, funding, claim or no-claim, response/silence, deadline, withdrawal,
   Record, email, stale-link, and wrong-account outcomes.
3. **Legal approval.** Software research can identify risks and design safer
   timing primitives, but it cannot approve real-money operation or replace
   jurisdiction-specific counsel.
4. **Private-document recovery.** A public event can prove that an evidence hash
   existed; it cannot reconstruct a lost private file or authorize a new reader.

## UX release recommendation

The remediated candidate is suitable for a supervised grant-reviewer testnet
demonstration once the matching contract cohort is signed, verified, configured,
and the focused hosted lifecycle retest passes. It is not ready for real deposits,
real tenancy documents, or unsupervised production use. No remaining visual polish
should be allowed to obscure an unresolved value, deadline, authorization, or
Record-reconciliation defect.
