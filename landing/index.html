How it works

Three clear steps. One shared record.

OpenEscrow organizes the deposit journey so each party can understand what happened, what comes next, and what information supports the outcome.

  1. 01

    Agree

    The parties review the property, deposit amount, timing, responsibilities, and local requirements before the agreement is finalized.

  2. 02

    Protect the deposit

    Testnet funding and important actions are tracked against the same agreement, without asking ordinary users to understand blockchain addresses or evidence pointers.

  3. 03

    Reach an outcome

    Unclaimed funds can return to the tenant. Claims include supporting information, and disagreements follow the applicable resolution process.

Why OpenEscrow

Built for people, with verifiable infrastructure underneath.

The experience leads with plain language. Technical receipts and detailed records are available when someone needs to verify what happened.

Shared understanding

Both sides see the same process

Proposals, active deposits, claims, responses, and outcomes stay connected instead of being scattered across texts, emails, and paper files.

Privacy with proof

Private details, public verification

Sensitive documents can remain encrypted while a timestamped receipt helps confirm that a record existed and has not been quietly replaced.

Requirements in context

Useful starting information by address

The MVP can prefill best-effort state guidance from official sources. The landlord remains responsible for confirming the rules that apply to the property.

Open source · Ethereum-powered receipts

Trust the process because it can be inspected.

OpenEscrow’s core software is available for public review. Ethereum is used as a shared receipt layer for selected actions—not as a reason to make the consumer experience more complicated.

The project is currently a public-interest prototype. It does not claim that code alone replaces legal compliance, professional review, or responsible operations.
